Curly Hair App

A UX research and app design project helping people with curly and natural hair build a personalized hair care routine, instead of piecing one together from scattered advice and trial and error.

Role
UX Research, UI Design
Type
Class Project
Timeline
4-week research sprint
Tools
Figma, Google Forms, Miro

The Problem

Hair care advice is everywhere, and nowhere at once

People with curly or natural hair often struggle to build a consistent hair care routine because information about products, techniques, and curl care is scattered across social media and other sources. As a result, users feel overwhelmed and unsure about what actually works for their specific hair type and goals.

User Goals

What users need from the app

  1. Receive personalized hair care routines based on curl type, texture, and hair goals.
  2. Discover products that work for their specific hair needs, and avoid ones that cause dryness or frizz.
  3. Learn simple styling techniques that are easy to follow and effective for maintaining curls.
  4. Track routines, products, and styling methods to identify what works best over time.
  5. Reduce stress and save time when making daily hair care decisions.

Scenarios

How each goal plays out in practice

Personalized Routines

Scenario

A new user answers questions about curl type, texture, and hair goals. The app generates a personalized routine for wash day and daily maintenance.

Success

The user receives a routine tailored to their hair profile and feels confident following a structured plan.

Product Recommendations

Scenario

A user searching for a leave-in conditioner that reduces frizz filters products by hair type and concerns to see personalized recommendations.

Success

The user finds a product that matches their needs and feels confident trying it.

Styling Tips

Scenario

A user wanting better curl definition opens a tutorial section with step-by-step instructions and visuals.

Success

The user follows the guide successfully and can recreate the style independently next time.

Tracking What Works

Scenario

After trying a new technique, the user logs the products used and the results in the app.

Success

The user reviews past routines and identifies which products and techniques worked best.

Research

Understanding the wash-day struggle

Before designing anything, I wanted to understand the daily struggles people with curly and natural hair face, how they currently choose products and routines, and what would actually be helpful in an app — not just what sounded good on paper.

Primary Audience

  • Ages 18–35
  • Curly, coily, or textured hair (mostly Type 2C–4C)
  • Interested in improving hair health or managing curls more easily
  • Uses multiple products or looks online for hair advice

Secondary Audience

  • Ages 25–45
  • Manages curly or natural hair at home
  • Relies on trial-and-error or DIY routines
  • Looking for an easier, more organized routine

Survey

A Google Forms survey (27 respondents) collected information on hair routines, frustrations, product preferences, and styling habits — e.g. how often users struggle choosing products, and where they typically get hair care advice.

User Interviews

Phone interviews explored frustrations with products and routines, emotional experiences with hair care, current habits and workarounds, and what users would want from a helpful app. Interviews were recorded and transcribed for analysis.

Competitive Analysis

Where existing apps fall short

I reviewed four existing hair care apps to see what was working, and where the gaps were. One happens to share a name with this project — "Competitor A" below is an unrelated app, not an earlier version of Curlè.

Category Competitor A Scrunchie Hair2Hair HairHub
AI Hair Scanning Accurate & fast Survey only Manual only Style only
Ingredient Analysis None None Detailed breakdowns None
Custom Routines Paid only Long survey Basic routine None
Weather Sync None Humidity check None None
Navigation Simple UI Too many steps Clear tabs Swipe UI
User Expectations High paywall Lengthy quiz Educational Fun, inspiring

01 — Reduce friction for immediate value

Strategy: Deliver AI scan results and a basic routine before any paywall.

Goal: Build user trust first, unlike Competitor A, which creates high drop-off by gating results immediately.

02 — Low-effort interaction model

Strategy: A gesture-based swipe interface for routine customization and product discovery.

Goal: Inspired by HairHub's intuitive navigation, this cuts the cognitive load of managing hair care for busy users like Jordan.

03 — An integrated care ecosystem

Strategy: Combine precision scanning, ingredient transparency, and environmental awareness in one place.

Goal: A single-source solution, so users don't have to juggle multiple apps to understand their hair's daily needs.

Research Summary

What the data showed

14% of respondents are happy with their current hair care routine
53% rarely try new products because of the cost of getting it wrong
64% struggle most with picking the right gels and creams
73% want an app that analyzes hair and weather to make routines easier
“

Bad experiences erode trust fast

Interview participants described products that left their hair "stiff and horrible" or "lumpy and sticky." Those experiences make people hesitant to try anything new, even when their current routine isn't working.

“

Wash day is a dreaded chore, not a ritual

Some routines take up to four hours. Participants described the process as "tiring" and "a pain," leading many to push wash day back rather than deal with it.

“

Users trust people over ads

Respondents said they trust TikTok, YouTube, and friends more than marketing, because they want proof from someone with hair that actually looks like theirs.

Information Architecture

Mapping the app before drawing a single screen

With the research findings in hand, I mapped the full structure of the app before sketching any screens. Two entry points — a returning user signing in, and a new user starting the AI hair scan — both resolve to one home dashboard, which branches into five clear areas: Home, Search & Shop, Scan, Journal, and Profile.

Keeping the scanner as its own center-tab destination — rather than burying it in a menu — reflects the research directly: scanning is the app's core value, so it needed to be reachable from anywhere in one tap.

Content Strategy

Real hair science, made simple

Core Message

"Real hair science made simple."

The app breaks complicated ingredient lists down into a simple match score, so users can quickly tell whether a product fits their hair type and porosity — without needing to research it themselves. The voice stays professional but laid-back: a guide that knows the facts without leaning on medical or scientific jargon.

Attribute Context Example
Expert Educational "This product contains heavy waxes. Since your hair is low-porosity, it may sit on top and cause buildup."
Helpful Encouraging "This is only a 50% match. Try the 'Better Match' option for something more hydrating."
Direct Urgent "It's very humid today. Use a sealing product to help reduce frizz."
Honest Relatable "This smells amazing, but it may not provide the moisture your hair needs today."

Task Analysis

Generating an AI hair profile

Goal

Give a new user an accurate hair classification in one simple flow, with zero guesswork.

User opens the app and selects "Analyze My Hair."

User follows on-screen prompts to position their camera.

User takes or uploads a clear photo of their hair texture.

The AI processes the image against hair classification data.

Success: the user sees their confirmed hair classification (e.g. Type 4A, High Porosity) — no manual quiz, no guessing.

Mapped as a flow

Diagramming the flow surfaced an edge case the step list glossed over: what happens when the photo isn't usable. The flowchart builds in a clarity check that loops the user back to retake the photo, instead of letting a bad scan silently produce a wrong hair profile.

Iteration

From sketch to interface

Every screen started on paper. Sketching the full flow by hand first — before opening Figma — kept the focus on structure and content over visual polish, and made it fast to throw ideas away.

The Solution

Walking through Curlè

The final flow answers each user goal from the research directly: a guided scan replaces guesswork, a match score replaces trial and error, and a daily log replaces relying on memory.

01 — Onboarding

A quick goal check, not a 6-minute quiz

Competitor apps like Scrunchie stretched onboarding past six minutes of questions. Curlè keeps it short, visual, and single-choice — addressing the "lengthy quiz" gap identified in the competitive analysis.

02 — AI Hair Scan

Confirming hair type with a photo, not a guess

This directly answers the task flow from the research phase: users capture a guided photo, and the app returns a confirmed classification — replacing the uncertainty Maya described when self-identifying her porosity and curl pattern.

03 — Instant Payoff

Value before any paywall

The strategy from the competitive analysis: give users their hair profile and first routine for free. Competitor A gates this behind a paywall, which research showed creates high drop-off and breaks trust before it's even built.

04 — Product Match Score

Ingredient science, in plain language

Solves the "guesswork problem" from the research summary directly: instead of reading a label and guessing, users get a compatibility score plus a short, jargon-free explanation of why an ingredient will or won't work for their specific porosity.

05 — Progress Journal

Tracking what actually works

Answers the "track what works over time" goal from research: a simple log of products, techniques, and results, so users can look back instead of relying on memory.
